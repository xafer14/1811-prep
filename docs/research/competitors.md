# Competitor and landscape research: 1811 Prep

Method: web search plus page fetches on 2026-11-24. Several sites (fbi.gov, dea.gov, atf.gov) returned HTTP 403 to fetch, so some facts come from search-result summaries only and are marked (unverified). SEO and "who ranks" notes are observational, from search results seen on one day.

## Summary
- No dedicated, multi-agency, free 1811 applicant prep site was found. Existing options split into: FBI-only paid test prep, single-agency official guides, a bare-bones volunteer blog, a Reddit community, and fitness-book/app sellers.
- Paid prep is FBI-centric. JobTestPrep FBI packs are $79 / $89 / $99 for 1 week / 1 month / 3 months (https://jobtestprep.com/fbi-special-agent-exam, accessed 2026-11-24).
- Sgt. Godoy packets are the most-recommended 1811 prep brand in affiliate-style pages; price not published in what I could reach (unverified).
- Official free material exists but is fragmented per agency: FBI app (free), DEA test prep guide, Secret Service study guide + APAT standards, ATF PTT page.
- Biggest apparent gap: no neutral side-by-side of agencies (age caps, tests, fitness standards, timelines, hiring windows) and no cross-agency fitness tracker.
- r/1811 is the main community; exists since at least 2022; mirrors show activity into 2025-2026.
- The specialagents.org blog is the closest content competitor (1811-focused, agent-run) but looks thin and affiliate-driven.
- Name "1811 Prep": no exact-match product, domain page, or social account surfaced in two searches (not a trademark clearance; domain availability not checked).
- Monetization seen: affiliate links to prep vendors, paid courses/packs, books, fitness memberships/coaching, job-board ads.
- Trust is a differentiator: the specialagents.org operator itself warns that most online posters are not agents (https://my.duda.co/site/cadac795, 2026-11-24).

### Q2. Free official resources and what they leave out
- FBI: free "FBI Physical Fitness Test" / "FitTest" app, mock PFT with trainer videos; free on App Store, developer FBI; simulator only, not official scoring (https://www.fbi.gov/fitnessapp via search summary; https://apps.apple.com/us/app/fbi-fittest/id1308329036, 2026-11-24). App Store showed only 2 ratings, average 3.0.
- FBI "All You Need to Know to Apply" guide, last revised April 2024 per search summary (https://testpp.fbijobs.gov/sites/default/files/how-to-apply.pdf) (unverified, not fetched).
- DEA: Special Agent Test Preparation Guide, a 2026 "Version 7" PDF exists (https://www.dea.gov/sites/default/files/2026-07/508_Special%20Agent%20Test%20Preparation%20Guide_Version%207.pdf); fetch blocked, content unverified.
- Secret Service: SAEE study guide PDF and published APAT standards (https://www.secretservice.gov/careers/special-agent/study-guide-and-fitness-standards, 2026-11-24). APAT figures: see fitness-standards-federal.md (superseded 2026-11-24).
- ATF: Pre-Employment Physical Task Test; applicants pay own travel to the test site; SAAA written test (search summary; https://www.atf.gov/careers/pre-employment-physical-task-test; fetch blocked, unverified).
- Gaps left: each agency documents only itself; no cross-agency comparison; no tracking of progress against standards; fitness standards are scattered across PDFs; no unified timeline (hiring windows, process length); little on non-headline agencies (NCIS, IRS-CI, DSS, HSI, etc.) in what I found.

### Q3. Gaps and positioning
- Multi-agency comparison tables (age cap, degree, tests, fitness test name, standards, polygraph, typical timeline, how/when posted).
- Readiness self-assessment that is explicitly educational, not a vendor funnel.
- PFT tracker that maps one set of reps/times to several agencies' scoring (FBI, USSS APAT, ATF PTT, DEA, etc.).
- Timeline guides honest about long processes (JobTestPrep says background and polygraph 6-12 months; another site says two years overall; sources unconfirmed by agencies).
- Clear sourcing and "last verified" dates versus anonymous forum claims and 2021-era testimonials.
- Neutral stance: not affiliated with any agency; no scraped test questions.

## Implications for the product
- Win on breadth (all agencies) and verified structure rather than on test-question banks.
- Make the PFT tracker the one interactive tool: single input set, per-agency scoring where official standards are published (USSS APAT numbers are published; verify FBI/DEA/ATF numbers from primary PDFs before building).
- Every number gets a source link and "verified on" date; this is the visible trust differentiator.
- Avoid competing with the FBI app on simulation; position as tracker plus planner.
- Do not host copyrighted or leaked test questions; vendors sell those.
- Content cadence matters because vendor pages show recency (JobTestPrep page dated Oct 2026).

## Decisions needed
1. Tool choice. Options: (a) PFT tracker, (b) readiness assessment, (c) both. Recommendation: (a) first; clear utility, repeat visits, local-storage only so $0 hosting works; assessment later. (superseded 2026-11-24: owner chose a hybrid fitness readiness tool, see ../interviews/02-product.md)
2. Scope of agencies at launch. Options: FBI only; top 5 (FBI, DEA, ATF, USSS, HSI); all. Recommendation: top 5 with a shared template, add others later.
3. Monetization stance. Options: none; affiliate disclosure; ads. Recommendation: defer; launch ad-free, add clearly disclosed affiliates only after traffic, and never for vendors you cannot vouch for.
4. Name. Options: keep "1811 Prep"; alternative. Recommendation: check domain and handles before committing; consider a descriptive tagline since "1811" is jargon to outsiders.
5. Domain/handle registration timing. Recommendation: reserve now, as it is cheap.

## Risks
- Official standards change and PDFs are blocked from automated fetch; stale tables harm trust. Mitigate with last-verified dates and review cadence.
- Appearing agency-affiliated; add a clear non-affiliation disclaimer.
- Scope creep across agencies for a solo owner.
- Vendor and forum figures (pass rates, applicant counts) are unverified; do not republish them as fact.
- Policy changes and hiring freezes (mentioned on r/1811 mirrors) can invalidate timeline content quickly.

## Contradictions
- FBI Phase I retake: one source says failing twice terminates the application; another says a retest is possible after 90 days but not guaranteed (search summary of third-party pages; JobTestPrep says 90 days, not all invited). Needs an FBI primary-source check.
- Process length: JobTestPrep timeline implies roughly 8-14+ months (screening 1-2 months, Phase I within 4-6 weeks, Phase II 1-2 months later, background/polygraph 6-12 months), while an agent-run site says at least two years. Different definitions likely.
- Phase I to Phase II average of 16 weeks (third-party) versus 1-2 months (JobTestPrep).
- Phase I pass rate "top 30%" is a third-party claim; the FBI reportedly describes selection as competitiveness-based, not a fixed cut (unverified).
- Stew Smith post says roughly 40,000 start applications and 400-500 hired per year; no primary source seen.
- Age cap: sources cite 37 (USSS, general retirement rule), 36 (DEA posting), and under 40 (DHS posting); these are agency-specific, not necessarily conflicting.
