# Fitness assessment validity (research for the 1811 Prep hybrid readiness tool)

All access dates are 2026-11-24. "Fetched" = page text read; "snippet" = only a search-result summary was seen, so treat as (unverified) until the paper is opened. Law-enforcement coverage is thin and mostly secondary; federal-LE-specific evidence was not found.

## Summary
- BMI is a poor individual-level predictor of body fat in young military-age adults, especially in the 25-30 range; it works better above ~30. Use it only as a coarse screen, never as a verdict.
- The policy ground is moving: DoD moved to waist-to-height ratio (WHtR < 0.55) as the upper limit from Jan 2026 and the Army reportedly dropped its height/weight tables and tape test (July 2026). A tool built on height/weight tables may be outdated. (secondary sources; primary DoD memo not read)
- Circumference/tape body-fat estimates differ from DXA by several points per person and track change poorly; they are a classification screen, not a measurement.
- The most consistent entry-level predictors of injury and success are run time (strongest for injury) and push-up/sit-up counts (weaker, more variable). Low-fit recruits who preconditioned before Army BCT completed more often than those who did not.
- Self-reported activity is a weak-to-moderate proxy for measured fitness; it cannot stand in for the user's own test results.
- Novice adaptation over 4-12 weeks varies widely; no controlled source found for typical run-time or push-up gains. Any time-to-ready number must be a wide range with a stated basis.
- Weekly "10% rule" lacks strong support; single-session spikes in the longest run are more strongly tied to overuse injury in one large cohort.
- ACSM's 2015 algorithm lets symptom-free, non-diseased people start light-to-moderate exercise without clearance; PAR-Q+ is the standard consumer questionnaire but has copyright/modified-copy limits.
- Numeric ranges communicate uncertainty with small trust cost; vague caveats cost more.
- Evidence base is mostly observational and male-skewed Army data; transfer to Marines, Navy, Air Force, federal LE is unproven.

## Findings

### Q1. BMI, body fat, and circumference methods
- US Army BCT trainees, Fort Jackson 2018-19, n=504 women and 965 men, DXA vs BMI: at BMI 25, predicted %BF fell from ~33% to ~29% (women) and ~23% to ~20% (men) between week 1 and week 9 (fetched, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10423487/, 2026-11-24).
- Same study: BMI 25 as a screen for high %BF had sensitivity 55-62% / specificity 69-79% (women) and 64-79% / 67-76% (men); authors call both "poor". BMI 27.5 raised specificity to ~91-98% but sensitivity fell to 9-15% (women) and 27-42% (men) (same URL, 2026-11-24).
- HPRC (DoD-affiliated, secondary) says BMI should not be a pass/fail measure and is fairly accurate above 30 (https://www.hprc-online.org/physical-fitness/training-performance/how-military-measures-body-composition, 2026-11-24).
- Opposite direction: a USUHS review argues the 25-29 BMI range in active-duty personnel may hide excess fat and suggests lower thresholds plus other measures (abstract only; https://scholar.usuhs.edu/en/publications/overweight-bmi-25-29-in-active-duty-military-excess-fat-or-more-l/, 2026-11-24, snippet).
- Circumference vs DXA, Army BCT, n=1,407 (481 women, 926 men): circumference %BF underestimated DXA by about 6 points at baseline in both sexes (-6.0 +/- 4.4 women, -6.0 +/- 3.5 men). Over BCT, DXA fell ~3-4 points; circumference showed 0 change in women and -2.2 in men. Authors say it may not suit tracking change (fetched, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10282178, 2026-11-24).
- Marine circumference equation vs DXA, n=430 men, 179 women: bias about -2.5 points for men; authors call it a field classification tool, not research-grade (snippet, https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fphys.2022.868627, 2026-11-24) (unverified).
- Army/USARIEM 2021 study, n=2,690: height/weight tables "correctly screened about 90%"; equation accuracy varied by body type; study publication pending; no tape-vs-DXA accuracy figure given (fetched, https://www.army.mil/article/273959/evolving_body_composition_standards_in_the_u_s_army_a_science_informed_approach, 2026-11-24).
- Policy (secondary): 18 Dec 2025 Personnel & Readiness memo set WHtR upper limit < 0.55 from 1 Jan 2026; those at/above get a service-specific body-fat assessment (https://www.militarytimes.com/news/pentagon-congress/2026/01/13/pentagon-to-start-measuring-troops-body-fat-by-waist-to-height-ratio, 2026-11-24, snippet; confirmed by https://www.militarytimes.com/news/your-military/2026/09/09/pentagon-codifies-fitness-standards-across-branches/, fetched, regulations published 31 Aug 2026). Army Times reports the Army dropped height/weight tables and tape test for semiannual WHtR (waist at navel / height) with a 180-day no-separation assessment period; start date and applicant applicability not stated (fetched, https://www.armytimes.com/news/your-military/2026/07/07/army-scraps-height-and-weight-tables-in-favor-of-waist-to-height-assessment/, 2026-11-24).

### Q2. Predictors of test pass, training success, injury
- Army IET men, Fort Benning, n=1,788 (retrospective): 1-mile run time was the strongest predictor of acute and overuse musculoskeletal injury; push-ups+sit-ups predicted acute injury only (~1% lower odds per extra rep). Authors' lowest-quartile flags: <19 push-ups, <26 sit-ups, run slower than 8:25 (fetched, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5224725, 2026-11-24). Men only; one site; injuries, not attrition.
- Army Fitness Assessment Program (Knapik 2006, Mil Med): recruits who failed entry fitness test. BCT completion: men 59% (no preconditioning, n=32), 83% (preconditioned, n=64), 87% (passed entry test, n=1,078); women 52% / 69% / 78%. Injury risk ratio vs passers: men 1.5-1.7, women 1.2-1.5 (fetched abstract, https://academic.oup.com/milmed/article-pdf/171/1/45/21978654/milmed.171.1.45.pdf, 2026-11-24). Small low-fit groups, non-randomized.
- Army "injury risk stabilized" at about a 17-minute 2-mile, 26 sit-ups, 10 push-ups for men (snippet, https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA426849.pdf, 2026-11-24) (unverified).
- Army 2026 article: passing body-composition standards associated with 30% lower risk of duty-limiting injury; ACFT 540+ total was protective even with higher body fat (fetched, army.mil/article/273959 above, 2026-11-24).
- Law enforcement (secondary): Massachusetts academy retrospective + prospective cohorts found push-ups and 1.5-mile run predicted graduation; fewer push-ups ~5x odds of failure, slower run ~4x (snippet, researchgate Merrigan link in sources, 2026-11-24) (unverified; abstract-level). FBI new-agent training: slower 1.5-mile and 300 m sprint associated with injury in men (snippet, researchgate 51701414, 2026-11-24, secondary).
- Contrasting LE study: southern California agency, n=401 (333 men, 68 women): no significant push-up differences between graduates and separated; 1.5-mile run not measured (fetched, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6388251, 2026-11-24).
- Graduation also depends on non-fitness factors (academics, discipline); fitness explains a minority of variance (193-recruit study: 18%, snippet, unverified).

### Q3. Self-reported activity vs measured fitness
- IPAQ-SF systematic review (Lee 2011): correlation with objective activity/fitness measures was below acceptable levels in the large majority of studies (search summary only; the page would not load, https://ijbnpa.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/1479-5868-8-115, 2026-11-24) (unverified).
- Example primary study (osteoarthritis patients; not our population): rho 0.11-0.46 vs accelerometer, under-reporting of total activity and over-reporting of moderate-vigorous (snippet, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7916302/, 2026-11-24).
- Finnish recruits: questionnaire items (activity, sitting, smoking, anthropometrics) together explained 52% of variance in a 12-minute run; authors say questionnaires do not accurately predict recruit fitness (snippet, https://jyx.jyu.fi/jyx/Record/jyx_123456789_47224, 2026-11-24) (unverified).
- No source found giving a simple single-item r with military test scores.

### Q4. Novice adaptation and progression
- No controlled study found giving typical 4-12 week gains in run time, push-ups, planks, or pull-ups for novices (searches 2026-11-24 returned only coaching plans and anecdotes, which are (secondary) and not used for numbers).
- Army BCT average improvement of ~56% (push-ups) and ~17% (2-mile) in men is a snippet whose metric (time vs score) is unclear (https://apps.dtic.mil/sti/pdfs/ADA426849.pdf, 2026-11-24) (unverified).
- ACSM 2009 stand: novices 8-12 RM loads, 2-3 days/week (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19204579/, 2026-11-24, snippet). ACSM 2011: gradual progression of intensity and volume may reduce exercise risk (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21694556/, 2026-11-24, snippet). ACSM released a 2026 resistance-training position stand updating 2009 guidance; contents only summarized via search (https://acsm.org/resistance-training-guidelines-update-2026/, 2026-11-24, snippet).
- Running volume: in 5,205 runners (18-month follow-up, observational), a single run >10% longer than the longest run in the prior 30 days raised overuse injury hazard (HR 1.64 for >10-30%); week-to-week change showed no significant association (fetched, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12421110, 2026-11-24). Population: mostly Europe/N America, mean age 45.8, not novices.
- A Groningen novice trial reportedly found ~20% injury in both +10%/week and +50%/week programs (secondary, snippet, 2026-11-24) (unverified).

### Q5. Pre-participation screening
- ACSM 2015 (Riebe et al., MSSE 47(11):2473-79): screening based on current activity level, known cardiovascular/metabolic/renal disease, signs/symptoms, and desired intensity; asymptomatic people without known disease may begin light-to-moderate exercise without medical clearance (https://journals.lww.com/10.1249/MSS.0000000000000851, 2026-11-24; algorithm details via secondary summaries; ACSM page returned 403). A comparison reportedly cut physician referrals 41% (secondary, https://www.acefitness.org/continuing-education/certified/february-2018/6898/new-preparticipation-guidelines-remove-barriers-to-exercise, 2026-11-24) (unverified).
- PAR-Q+: 7 general questions; all "No" = cleared for unrestricted activity; any "Yes" leads to follow-up questions and, if still "Yes", ePARmed-X+ and/or a qualified exercise professional. Site shows a 2025 fillable form and "Copyright PAR-Q+ Collaboration 2007-2026", says "avoid outdated or modified copies"; embedding terms not found (fetched, https://eparmedx.com/, 2026-11-24). Origin: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3596208/ (2026-11-24, snippet).

### Q6. Presenting uncertainty
- Five experiments (n=5,780): numeric ranges signal uncertainty with little loss of trust; verbal-only caveats cost more (search summary of the PNAS 2020 paper; not fetched, 2026-11-24) (secondary).
- Van der Bles 2019 review: stating uncertainty does not necessarily undermine trust; about half of respondents in one study read the top of a range as the true value; showing a best estimate plus a visual helps; verbal terms ("likely") are read inconsistently; numbers beside words improve alignment (fetched, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6549952, 2026-11-24; trust section was not read).

## Algorithm design implications

DO
- Make the primary body-composition check follow the current official method per service (WHtR < 0.55 where adopted); show height/weight screening only for services/dates where it is still verified, with the policy date visible.
- Show a "measurement method, source, and as-of date" line for every standard used; re-verify against each service's current regulation.
- Compare self-test results to official minimums event by event; show pass/fail per event and the margin.
- Treat self-test inputs as self-reported and unverified; ask test conditions (surface, form standard, rest).
- Give time-to-ready as a range with a stated basis, labeled "rough estimate, not a prediction".
- Show the best-estimate point inside the range plus a simple visual; avoid vague words like "likely".
- Widen the range when inputs are weaker (self-reported activity only, one data point, big gap to the standard, no retest).
- Offer retest prompts (e.g., re-enter results after 2-4 weeks) and update the range from actual progress.
- Use run time as the heaviest-weighted "injury caution" signal if any flag is shown; frame as "ramp up gradually", not a risk score.
- Keep progression guidance generic and conservative: cap the longest single run increase, favor gradual volume.

DON'T
- Don't use BMI as a pass/fail or "overweight/healthy" judgment, and don't imply BMI = body fat.
- Don't show a tape/circumference body-fat percentage as a measured fact, or use it to track weekly change.
- Don't use self-reported activity to estimate current event performance or pass odds; use it only for the labeled time-to-ready range.
- Don't promise a date, "guaranteed ready", or per-week gains drawn from anecdotes.
- Don't use the 10%-per-week rule as a validated safe threshold.
- Don't display clinical language (diagnose, risk of disease, cardiac clearance, "medically safe").
- Don't claim fitness predicts graduation or selection; fitness is one of several factors.
- Don't copy or alter PAR-Q+ text without checking the PAR-Q+ Collaboration terms.

## Safety and screening

Show before any training suggestion (a gate, not a footnote):
1. Plain statement: "This tool is educational, not medical advice. It cannot tell you if exercise is safe for you."
2. A short readiness screen. Preferred: link/embed the official PAR-Q+ (once license is confirmed); fallback: ACSM-style questions on (a) known heart, metabolic (e.g., diabetes), or kidney disease; (b) symptoms during activity (chest pain/pressure, unusual shortness of breath, dizziness or fainting); (c) current activity level; (d) intended intensity.
3. Outcome rules: any known-disease or symptom "Yes" shows "Talk to a doctor or qualified exercise professional before starting" and withholds the training plan and time estimate; all "No" allows light-to-moderate starts with gradual progression (per ACSM 2015, secondary-summary basis).
4. Standing stop-signs: stop and seek care for chest pain, fainting, severe breathlessness, or sharp joint/bone pain.
5. Heat, hydration, and ramp-up reminder; note that sudden big jumps in single-run length raise overuse injury.
6. Dated, recorded acknowledgement; PAR-Q+ is valid 12 months per one secondary source, so re-prompt at least yearly (unverified; eparmedx.com states no validity period).
7. Pregnancy/postpartum, under-18, and age-specific cases: not researched; route to professional (see Open questions).

## Decisions needed
1. Body-composition method. Options: (a) WHtR as primary, height/weight table as legacy display; (b) height/weight tables only; (c) service-by-service switch with "as of" dates. Recommendation: (c), defaulting to WHtR < 0.55 where a service has adopted it, after reading the primary DoD memo and each service's regulation.
2. Screening gate. Options: (a) embed PAR-Q+; (b) custom 5-6 question ACSM-style screen; (c) link out only. Recommendation: (b) at launch with a link to PAR-Q+; move to (a) only if the collaboration permits embedding.
3. Time-to-ready output. Options: (a) single date; (b) range in weeks with label; (c) no estimate. Recommendation: (b), min-max in weeks, shown only after the screen passes, with a "what widens this range" note.
4. Federal LE scope. Options: (a) include only agencies whose standards are read from official source; (b) military only at launch. Recommendation: (b) until agency-specific standards and validity evidence (not found here) are collected.
5. Optional run-time "injury caution" flag. Options: include or omit. Recommendation: omit the flag; keep a general gradual-ramp message, since cutoffs come from men-only, single-site data.

## Risks
- Outdated standards: body-composition rules changed in 2026 and may change again (Army 180-day assessment period; services submit reports from 1 Jun 2027).
- Users read an estimate as a promise or a medical clearance.
- Anecdote-level adaptation numbers could leak into the model.
- Self-test inputs may be inflated (form, rounding), producing false "ready" results.
- Evidence skews to young Army men; women and other services under-represented (women's sample sizes are smaller in cited studies).
- Skipping or misreading the screen for users with undiagnosed cardiac conditions.
- Using the PAR-Q+ without permission.
- Pre-exercise "low fitness" framing may discourage candidates or push them to over-train.

## Contradictions
- BMI direction of error: BCT data (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10423487/) say BMI 25 is outdated and over-flags lean trainees; USUHS and a PLoS ONE study (snippets) say BMI misses fat in military personnel. Both can hold: poor individual precision either way. Not resolved.
- Tape direction: Army BCT study (-6 points vs DXA) and Marine data (-2.5, snippet) show underestimation; a 2013 Military Times dunk-tank piece (journalism, https://www.militarytimes.com/2013/05/21/experts-tape-test-has-huge-margin-of-error/) reports overestimation in 9 of 10. Different methods, populations, and rigor; not resolved.
- Army policy: army.mil (273959) describes the 2023 single-site tape test as current and says height/weight tables screened ~90% correctly; Army Times (July 2026) says tables and tape test were dropped for WHtR. Likely a timing difference; primary Army regulation not read.
- HPRC (secondary) says "military uses WHtR as of 1 Jan 2026" while its own editor's note says DoDI 1308.03 is still being revised; Army has a 180-day no-separation period. Effective dates vary by source.
- Push-up predictive value: Massachusetts academies (snippet) report ~5x odds; the California agency study found no significant push-up differences; Army IET found only a small acute-injury effect. Outcome (graduation vs injury) and population differ.
- 10% rule: not supported as a weekly cap in the 5,205-runner cohort, yet 20-60% weekly jumps linked to more injuries at 21 days in one secondary table. Not resolved.
- Uncertainty communication: Van der Bles review and PNAS summary say ranges cost little trust; a cited crisis-communication review notes Kreps & Kriner found ranges can undermine trust (secondary, not read).

## Open questions
- What do the primary DoD memo (Dec 2025/Jan 2026), DoDI 1308.03 revision, and each service's current regulation say for applicants (accession) versus serving members?
- Do accession/recruit screens (MEPS height/weight) still use tables while serving members use WHtR?
- Which federal LE agencies (FBI PFT, FLETC, CBP, USSS, etc.) have published standards, and is there any validity evidence for their tests?
- Typical 4-12 week gains in run time/push-ups/planks/pull-ups for novices: need a controlled source (full text of Knapik 2006, ADA426849, trials of bodyweight training).
- Self-report: obtain full text of Lee 2011 and the Finnish recruit studies for actual r values.
- Women's and under-21 data; PAR-Q+ embedding license; pregnancy/postpartum and minors rules.
- Whether run-time-based thresholds from 1-mile Army tests transfer to 1.5-mile or 2-mile tests.
